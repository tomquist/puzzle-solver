# Charge once, right after the first "aha"

Puzzle Solver should make money from a **one-time "Pro" unlock, about €12.99 / $12.99 (with a €9.99 founder price at launch)**, plus a **non-renewing 30-day "Puzzle Pass" at about €4.99** that mostly works as a decoy to make the lifetime unlock look like the obvious choice. A **free tier limited by quantity, not features** (for example 3 piece-scans per puzzle, unlimited puzzles, fit animations included) puts the paywall right after the user first sees the app work. Payment should go through a **Merchant of Record** such as Polar, Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, with a small serverless function that issues **signed licence tokens the PWA checks offline**. The case rests on four strands of evidence. Usage is episodic, so the subscription advantage seen in app-store benchmarks mostly disappears: low-priced apps earn only about $8–11 per payer in year one. The category's price anchors sit between $0.99/month and a $19.99 lifetime. Ads and tip jars produce trivial revenue at niche scale and would contradict the privacy pitch. Puzzle Solver's batch scanning of 10–40 pieces, piece-to-piece fit, and handling of assembled patches are features no artwork-matching competitor offers. The category appeared only in 2026: at least five one-person apps launched, none has more than about 5K installs, and none has published revenue. So the realistic goal is a few hundred to low thousands of euros a month, earned by owning the niche before clones arrive. Before charging, the owner must fix three things that the research turned up as blockers or risks: **choose a licence for the public repo**, which currently has no LICENSE; **move off GitHub Pages to a custom domain**, before users build up data in IndexedDB on the old origin; and **check community sentiment on Reddit**, which the researchers could not reach.

## A brand-new, crowded micro-category where nobody has proven demand

The physical-jigsaw assistant category is a few months old. Competitor research found five artwork-matching apps and one shape-matching app, all by solo developers, and none from a manufacturer or a funded startup. Three iOS apps launched within ten days of each other in September 2026. **PuzzleLens** sells a **$0.99/month** ad-free tier ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/puzzlelens-puzzle-solver/id6812349555)). **Puzzle Detective** sells **$4.99 for 3 months, $9.99/year or $19.99 lifetime** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/puzzle-detective-piece-finder/id6808541265)). **Puzzle Pointer** gives 5 free searches, then sells **10 searches for $2.99 or unlimited for $6.99/month** ([puzzlepointer.com](https://puzzlepointer.com/)). On Android, Solver Works' **Jigsaw Puzzle Solver** is free with ads, at **5K+ installs and 2.8 stars** ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=puzzle.solver.puzzle_solver_app&hl=en_US)). LY4Dev's **Puzzle Finder** is free with a Ko-fi link, also at 5K+ ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=www.ly.puzzlefinder&hl=en_US)). **PuzPal** promises a one-time price that it has not published, and it does not appear to be live yet ([PuzPal](https://puzpal.com/about/)). A near-identical on-device iPhone PWA, **jellojm/puzzle-helper**, appeared on GitHub in October 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/jellojm/puzzle-helper)). The iOS launches have 0–1 ratings each. There is **no published revenue or download estimate for any of them**, so nobody has yet shown that people will pay for this.

On features, the gap in Puzzle Solver's favour is wide. Every artwork-matching competitor finds **one piece per photo**. None of them matches pieces to each other or handles assembled patches. Only PuzPal does fit matching, and only by contour, one piece at a time ([competitor comparison sources](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/puzzle-pointer-piece-finder/id6809997519)). Two features are no longer unique: rotation guidance (PuzzleLens and Puzzle Pointer have it) and warnings about hard pieces. Privacy is a real marketing point. **Puzzle Pointer uploads photos to its server and sends a copy to OpenAI** ([privacy policy](https://puzzlepointer.com/privacy/)). **PuzzleLens markets itself as on-device yet declares tracking identifiers** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/puzzlelens-puzzle-solver/id6812349555)). Solver Works' ad SDK shares location and device IDs ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=puzzle.solver.puzzle_solver_app&hl=en_US)). The one sizeable body of reviews, about 50 for Solver Works, shows that **accuracy on photographic and low-detail areas ("blue skies") decides ratings**. One reviewer put it at "less than 25% correct" and asked for a "try again" loop. This matters for monetization: charging for a tool that is confidently wrong on the sky produces refunds and 1-star reviews.

The audience is large and the market is stable. Paywalled analyst estimates put global jigsaw sales at **about US$2.2–2.8B in 2025, growing 3–5% a year** ([Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/index.php/puzzle-markt-110601); [SkyQuest](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/sky1917151-jigsaw-puzzle-market-size-share-growth-analysis-by.html)). Company reports are more solid: **Ravensburger's core games/puzzles/books business grew 3% in 2025**, adult puzzles were a "bright spot", and US/Canada sales rose 11% ([Spielwarenmesse](https://www.spielwarenmesse.de/en/mag/toy-market-news/ravensburger-2025-growth-in-core-business-drop-in-trading-cards/)). In the only good survey, **48% of US adults puzzle at least yearly and 19% monthly**. Frequent puzzling skews *younger and male* (34% of 18–34s versus 10% of over-55s; 24% of men versus 14% of women), so the common assumption that the audience is mostly older women holds only for *buyers*, and only on unverified aggregator data ([Ipsos/Ravensburger, 2019](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/node/478706)). The online community is mid-sized: **r/Jigsawpuzzles has about 126K members**, growing about 23% a year ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/Jigsawpuzzles/)).

## Why a one-time unlock beats subscriptions, ads and tips for this product

App-store subscription benchmarks are the strongest data available. Read carefully, they favour a one-time unlock here. RevenueCat finds that **hard paywalls convert 10.7–12.1% of downloads versus about 2.1% for freemium**, and earn about 8x more revenue per install ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [SaaStr summary](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)). **Half of all paid conversions happen on Day 0.** The lesson is to ask for payment early, while the user is impressed. A recurring charge is not part of that lesson. The same data shows that **low-priced apps realise only about $10.69 of year-1 LTV per payer**, against $62.19 for high-priced apps. About **72% of annual subscribers turn off auto-renew in year one** ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Weekly plans, which dominate Utilities revenue, keep only **3.4% of subscribers after a year** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). A puzzler who uses the app intensively for a week or two and then not again for a month would hit renewal between puzzles, which is the worst moment for it. Puzzle Solver also has **no server costs**, which removes the usual argument for subscriptions ("we pay for every search"). That argument does push Puzzle Pointer toward metered pricing. A single unlock at €10–13 captures about the same amount as the year-1 LTV of a low-priced subscription, without the resentment of a renewal, and it matches the "no backend, nothing to rent" story.

The strongest counter-argument is that subscriptions compound and one-time sales do not. Indie evidence suggests this matters less than it seems in small utilities. One developer who offered monthly, yearly and lifetime plans reports that **"most paid users choose yearly or lifetime"** ([dabo.dev](https://dabo.dev/revealing-my-revenue-metrics)). **Halide** kept a lifetime option next to its subscription when it switched models ([Lux](https://www.lux.camera/why-is-halide-not-free/)). More than **35% of apps already mix subscriptions with lifetime or consumable purchases** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). If revenue later needs to recur, the better lever is paid major versions or new content, such as hint modes or puzzle-specific features. An auto-renewing plan on an episodic tool is the wrong lever.

Ads and donations are not worth building. Hobby-site AdSense earns roughly **$0.40–$5 per 1,000 pageviews** ([AppAdRevenue](https://www.appadrevenue.com/guides/how-much-do-websites-earn)), and a single-page tool used in long sessions produces few pageviews. The research notes estimate about **$100/month from 10,000 monthly users**. Third-party ad scripts would also undercut the one claim competitors cannot match. Tip jars convert at about **0.015–0.023%** in documented anecdotes ([Convopage](https://convopage.com/c/1269349151483011072); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/1-4206-conversion-rate-on-buy-me-a-coffee-1ed21a2288)). Open pay-what-you-want pricing increased the number of buyers in the classic Gneezy field experiment, but **the average payment dropped to about $0.92** ([Freakonomics summary](https://freakonomics.com/2010/07/how-to-maximize-pay-what-you-wish-pricing)), and a fixed low price can sell to more people than naming your own price ([PNAS 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3358869)). A "Finished! Buy me a coffee" link after a completed puzzle is harmless as an extra. It is not a business.

| Model | Rough revenue per 10,000 activated users | Fit with Puzzle Solver | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-time Pro unlock (2–4% × ~€9.80 net) | ~€2,000–4,000 per cohort | Strong: episodic use, no running costs, privacy story | Medium: conversion is inferred from app-store benchmarks, not measured on PWAs |
| Annual subscription, low price | ~€1,600–2,200 in year 1, then heavy churn | Weak: renewal falls between puzzles | Strong for mobile benchmarks; episodic fit is inferred |
| Per-search packs (Puzzle Pointer style) | Unclear | Poor: a 40-piece batch would cost about $12 at Pointer's pack rate | Competitor pricing only |
| Ads | ~$100/month at 10K MAU | Poor: contradicts privacy-first | Medium (operator RPM surveys) |
| Tips / donations | Single-digit tips per month | Supplement only | Anecdotal but consistent |
| Puzzle-retailer affiliate links | Tens to low hundreds of € per year realistically | Supplement only | Commission rates verified; click-through rate is assumed |

The revenue figures in the table are my own arithmetic from the benchmark ranges above. They are not measured outcomes. The affiliate row deliberately falls well below the research notes' upper bound of about $8,000/year, because that bound assumes every user buys one puzzle a year through the app.

## Concrete pricing, free-tier boundary and paywall design

The offer should have two paid products shown next to each other, with lifetime visually dominant. Superwall customers report that adding a discounted longer plan next to a short one raised revenue per user substantially. This is anecdotal ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/solutions/by-stage/existing-apps)). Prices sit inside the competitor range of $0.99/month to $19.99 lifetime. Puzzle Detective's $19.99 lifetime is the closest comparable, and Puzzle Solver offers more features, so a price below it is a deliberate choice to win the category early rather than a ceiling.

| Product | Price (EUR/USD, incl. VAT) | Approx. net after 19% VAT and 5% + $0.50 MoR fee | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Puzzle Solver Pro, lifetime** | €12.99 / $12.99 standard; **€9.99 / $9.99 founder price** for the first ~60 days or first 500 buyers | ~€9.80 (standard), ~€7.40 (founder) | The product most buyers should choose |
| **Puzzle Pass, 30 days, non-renewing** | €4.99 / $4.99 | ~€3.50 | Decoy and low-commitment option for one-off puzzlers |
| Tip after completion | Free choice | — | Goodwill extra |
| Regional pricing (phase 3) | ~40–50% lower in lower-income markets | — | Benchmarks show 2–3x price gaps between countries ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)) |

The net figures are my calculation, assuming German VAT and $0.50 ≈ €0.46. They show why the cheap tier should not go below about €4. **Fixed MoR fees take 10–17% of a €3–5 sale** ([aliteq](https://aliteq.com/cheapest-merchant-of-record)). A 30-day pass also verifies cleanly offline, because a signed token carries its expiry date. A "per-puzzle credit" would be easy to reset by clearing storage.

The free tier should be measured in **quantity, never in features**. The recommended starting point is **3 piece-scans per puzzle on unlimited puzzles**, with every feature available during those scans: placement, rotation, fit animations, patches and guided placement. Because each scan covers 10–40 pieces, 3 scans locate 30–120 pieces. Puzzle Pointer gives away 5 single pieces. That makes Puzzle Solver look generous next to competitors while still putting the paywall inside the first session, where **50% of conversions happen** ([SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)). Keeping the fit animation free matters for a second reason: it is the shareable visual that drives organic video. The obvious alternative is "your first puzzle is fully free". That transfers the long-trial finding (**17–32 day trials convert at 42.5% versus 25.5% for short trials**, [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)) into episodic terms, but it pushes the paywall to the next puzzle, weeks later, after the excitement has faded. Run it as an A/B arm, not the default. No source tests usage-limited versus time-limited free tiers for episodic tools, so this is a reasoned starting point and the owner's own data must settle it. Two goodwill rules apply whatever the gate: **never gate export or the user's own stored puzzles**, and do not refuse a scan the app judges unusable, as Puzzle Pointer already does. Gating early carries a refund cost: hard paywalls refund at **5.8% versus 3.4% for freemium** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). That argues for showing the paywall *after* a successful scan, never before one.

The paywall copy should anchor the price against the puzzle itself, along the lines of "less than the puzzle on your table". It should state the privacy promise ("pay once, nothing uploaded, no account, no subscription"), and it should present Pro as help when stuck rather than a solver, for the reasons in the next sections.

## Payment, licensing and legal mechanics for a backend-less EU seller

Use a **Merchant of Record**. The MoR becomes the legal seller and handles VAT for each EU country, invoices, and the German withdrawal-waiver checkout. For a paid download, German consumers keep a **14-day right of withdrawal unless they expressly consent to immediate delivery, acknowledge losing the right, and receive confirmation on a durable medium** ([IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/widerrufsrecht-digitale-inhalte-dienstleistungen-2022.html)). A court has held that a passive store notice does not count as that consent ([LG Köln](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/lg-koeln-widerrufsrecht-verzicht-digitale-inhalte.html?print=1)). Plain Stripe is cheaper per sale, but the owner would then have to build all of this and register for EU VAT himself. Third-party comparisons put **Paddle at 5% + $0.50 flat, Polar at 5% + $0.50 on its free plan (some sources say 4% + $0.40), and Lemon Squeezy at 5% + $0.50 plus surcharges for international cards and PayPal** ([aliteq](https://aliteq.com/cheapest-merchant-of-record); [delivvo](https://delivvo.io/blog/polar-vs-lemonsqueezy-vs-paddle-merchant-of-record-2026)). Lemon Squeezy is being folded into **Stripe Managed Payments**, whose pricing sources disagree on ([fungies](https://fungies.io/lemon-squeezy-stripe-acquisition-saas-founders-2026/)). **Polar documents a licence-key API** with activation limits and expiry ([Polar docs](https://polar.sh/docs/features/benefits/license-keys)), which makes it the pragmatic first choice. Paddle is the conservative alternative. All rates come from third-party sites, so check them on the vendors' own pricing pages.

For the unlock, use a **signed-token design** rather than calling a licence API from the client. A single Cloudflare Worker, or similar, does two things. It receives the MoR's purchase webhook or a post-checkout redirect, confirms the order with the MoR's API, and returns a token `{orderId, emailHash, tier, issuedAt, expiresAt}` signed with an **Ed25519** private key. The PWA embeds the public key and verifies the token with WebCrypto, **fully offline**. Restoring a purchase works by entering the purchase email or licence key, which the Worker exchanges for a new token. No accounts are needed. This adds a "backend" only for issuing tokens; the app itself still runs entirely on-device. Two iOS details make easy restore essential. Installed home-screen PWAs keep **storage separate from Safari**, so a purchase made in the browser does not carry over to the installed app. Site data can also be evicted ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)). Do not invest in anti-piracy: all the code runs on the client, so any gate can be patched out. Binding tokens to a hashed order ID makes leaked tokens traceable, which is enough ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-tried-to-pirate-my-own-product-799871fa0a)). Several points are not verified and need checking: whether the Polar and Lemon Squeezy licence endpoints allow browser (CORS) calls, and current browser support for WebCrypto Ed25519.

**Hosting and the repository are the two prerequisites the research notes did not cover.** Both claims below come from my background knowledge and should be checked. First, GitHub's documentation states that **GitHub Pages is not intended to run an online business, e-commerce site or commercial SaaS** ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/about-github-pages)). A paid unlock on `tomquist.github.io` is therefore exposed to that policy. Moving to a custom domain on Cloudflare Pages or Netlify fixes that. It also provides the `/.well-known/assetlinks.json` file that a Google Play TWA needs at the domain root, which a GitHub project page cannot serve ([distribution notes](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en-GB)). And it puts the token Worker on the same domain. **Do this before any marketing.** Puzzles live in IndexedDB, which belongs to one origin, so every user acquired on the old domain would have to use Settings → Export and re-import after a move. Second, a public repo with **no LICENSE is "all rights reserved" by default**: others may view and fork it on GitHub but have no right to reuse it ([choosealicense.com](https://choosealicense.com/no-permission/)). That gives some legal protection but no practical protection. With at least five clones of the concept in 2026, some apparently built quickly by AI-assisted coding, the computer-vision pipeline in a public repo is free R&D for competitors. The recommended path is to **make the repo private, or publish under a source-available non-commercial licence such as PolyForm Noncommercial**, and keep the paid build closed. A fully open-source model (AGPL plus paid convenience) is a legitimate choice if the owner values reputation over revenue, but it caps what a paid tier can earn. Avoid the brand name as well: **Solver Works already uses "Jigsaw Puzzle Solver" and `puzzlesolver.app`** ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=puzzle.solver.puzzle_solver_app&hl=en_US)). A distinctive name makes SEO, store listings and any trademark filing far easier.

On German administration, the MoR pays out as a B2B sale from one company. The **Kleinunternehmer limits are €25,000 previous-year and €100,000 current-year turnover** ([IHK München](https://www.ihk-muenchen.de/ratgeber/steuern/umsatzsteuer/kleinunternehmerregelung/)). The owner still needs an Impressum and a short privacy notice, which is easy for an app with no tracking. How MoR payouts count toward the Kleinunternehmer limits and reverse-charge reporting is unresolved and should go to a Steuerberater.

## Distribution: web first, then Google Play, then iOS only if earned

A web-only product cannot use Apple Search Ads, app-install campaigns or store search. Store search is where every competitor gets found. The store routes have become cheap for small developers. **Google Play charges 10% on the first $1M, plus 5% only if Play Billing is used**, since 30 June 2026 in the US, UK and EEA ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/06/24/google-play-store-external-billing-june-30/)). **Apple's EU terms from 1 October 2026 charge Small Business Program members 15% for in-app purchase and 10% for link-outs** ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/)). The obstacles are practical. A new personal Play account must run a **closed test with 12 testers for 14 days** before it can publish ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en-GB)). On iOS, a thin web wrapper is the textbook **guideline 4.2 rejection** ([technetexperts](https://www.technetexperts.com/guideline-4-2-minimum-functionality/)). Puzzle Solver is better placed than most wrappers, because it works offline and uses the camera on-device. A Capacitor build that bundles the assets locally and adds native camera capture, haptics and a share sheet has a credible chance of approval. That is an inference: a Capacitor app with Swift plugins was still rejected in January 2026 ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889)).

The resulting order is: web checkout first (lowest fees, all platforms reached at once); a **Google Play TWA next**, which is cheap reach, using Play Billing for simplicity at 15% (whether TWAs must use Play Billing is unverified); and an **iOS App Store build only after web data shows that iOS users convert**. iOS is where installing a PWA is hardest, needing four or more taps with no install prompt ([MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/progressive-web-apps-ios)), so the payoff there is largest. It also costs the most and is the least stable: Apple's 0% commission on US link-outs depends on a Supreme Court case Apple has appealed ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/06/30/high-court-grants-cert-in-apples-challenge-to-ninth-circuit-contempt-ruling-in-app-store-dispute/)). Inside the stores, use store billing at these prices. Combining a link-out commission with an MoR fee costs more than either alone.

## Marketing and partnerships: demo video and the "stuck on the sky" pitch

No acquisition case study exists for a jigsaw-helper app, so channel ranking is inference built on analogues. **Paid acquisition is out at launch.** iOS utility installs cost **$1.20–$3.50** and Apple Search Ads acquisitions about **$2.50–$2.90** ([SEMNexus](https://semnexus.com/cpi-benchmarks-app-category-platform-2026/); [App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/apple-ads-search-results-benchmarks-report-2025/)), while a €10–13 unlock at a 2–4% conversion rate earns about €0.20–0.50 per activated user. The cheapest channel is **organic short video** on TikTok, Reels and Shorts. The fit animation, in which a piece lifts, turns and clicks into place, is exactly the kind of instant visual payoff behind the indie Dumbphone case: **about 2.4M TikTok views, 20K downloads and $9K MRR in 78 days**, from simple handheld screen recordings. That figure comes from a secondary source without primary confirmation ([Shortimize](https://shortimize.com/blog/20k-downloads-and-9k-mrr-in-78-days-how-this-indie-app-turned-minimalism-into-millions-of-views)). Jigsaw creators exist at **100K–215K followers** (@mjpuzzles25 about 214.7K, [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/mjpuzzles25/); Karen Puzzles 200K+ on YouTube, [Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/life/holidays-and-celebrations/meet-the-woman-who-turned-finishing-puzzles-into-her-career)). No one has made a piece-finder app go viral, so the first credible account can own the niche, but nothing shows the audience wants this. Public sponsorship rates do not exist. Gifting free Pro codes to micro and mid-sized creators (5K–50K) is the realistic route.

The messaging must handle the **"cheating" stigma**. Puzzlers say they do it for relaxation and fun ([Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/node/478706)). Digital-jigsaw communities argue over whether rotation hints are cheating, and Jigsaw Mania lets players switch off features that "might be considered cheating" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/351030/discussions/0/1291817837620862241); [Inertia Software](https://inertiasoftware.com/jigsaw/help/wiz4.htm)). Every competitor positions itself as "an assistant, not a solver", and PuzzleLens tracks how many hints each puzzle took. Lead with four rescue stories: **"Stuck on the sky?"**, **"Lost the box?"** (vintage and second-hand puzzles, which fits Karen Puzzles' vintage content), **accessibility** (low vision, shaky hands, helping a parent finish a puzzle; avoid health claims), and **sorting** ("which of these 40 pieces fit together?"). Back the message with product changes: a region-only hint level, a "no spoilers" mode, and a "you placed 940 of 1,000 pieces yourself" statistic. Graded hints also give Pro something to offer that is not just "more scans".

The supporting channels, in order, are as follows. **SEO landing pages** for high-intent, low-competition queries such as "find where a puzzle piece goes" and "jigsaw without the box picture"; the search results today show only small apps and listicles, though there is no volume data. **Reddit and Facebook**, used mainly for listening, plus one maker post that leads with a demo GIF and asks honestly whether the tool would spoil the hobby. r/puzzles allows self-promotion only in a weekly thread ([wiki mirror](https://reddit.fsky.io/r/puzzles/wiki/index)), and r/Jigsawpuzzles' rules could not be read. **Show HN**, built on the on-device WASM computer vision and "your photos never leave your phone", which brings backlinks and tech-press pickup rather than puzzlers. Product Hunt launches typically bring **500–1,000 visitors**, and the median indie launch gets 3 upvotes ([DEV](https://dev.to/holiney/is-product-hunt-dead-or-what-results-can-you-expect-from-product-hunt-launch-4j3d); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-median-indie-launch-last-week-got-3-upvotes-d1c2406a1f)). **Puzzle retailers**: Rest in Pieces already promotes a puzzle app on its blog ([Rest in Pieces](https://www.restinpieces.co.uk/blogs/news/jigsaw-puzzle-tracker-app)), and a "stuck? scan here" QR card in shipped orders costs a retailer almost nothing.

Partnerships carry upside but no revenue the owner should count on. **Affiliate links** pay **3% at Amazon (Toys)** and **4–8% at puzzle retailers** ([Lasso](https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/); [Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/72473); [affi.io](https://affi.io/m/puzzle-at)). That is $0.45–2 per $15–25 puzzle, so a "your next puzzle" link on the completion screen is a small extra. It is only outbound links, so it is compatible with the privacy promise if disclosed. The most interesting B2B lead is **Ravensburger**. Its replacement service for puzzles of 9000+ pieces already asks customers for the **grid reference** of the missing piece ([Ravensburger support](https://ravensburger-en.mindtouch.us/Service_and_Support/Faulty_Items/Missing_a_puzzle_piece_(9000__piece_puzzles))), which Puzzle Solver could generate automatically. The company is also investing in speed-puzzling events and digital printing. Pitch it only once there is usage data. **Rental services and community puzzle libraries** have a real problem checking that returned puzzles are complete, but they run on charity-sized budgets ([Artifact Puzzles](https://www.artifactpuzzles.com/products/6-month-puzzle-club-subscription); [Mary Stevens Hospice](https://www.marystevenshospice.co.uk/stourbridge-jigsaw-library)). **Care homes** use 13–100 piece puzzles, where the app adds little ([Training2Care](https://www.training2care.com/products/activities/les-ives-family-dementia-jigsaw.htm)). Use accessibility for press and award entries, not as a sales line.

## Sequenced plan, metrics and kill criteria

The plan needs measurement, and the privacy promise limits it. Use **cookieless, identifier-free aggregate event counters** (for example a count endpoint on the same Worker), disclose them in the privacy notice, and confirm the setup under German device-access rules with a lawyer. The MoR dashboard supplies sales, refunds and country mix. The metric targets below are starting assumptions derived from the benchmarks. They are not predictions.

| Phase | Timing | Actions | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Foundations** | Weeks 0–3 | Decide the repo licence and visibility; choose a distinctive brand and buy the domain; move hosting off GitHub Pages; add Impressum and privacy notice; build the token Worker plus offline verify, restore flow and paywall; add a "wrong spot, try next" loop and graded-hint mode; add aggregate counters; benchmark accuracy on sky and photographic puzzles against PuzzleLens, Puzzle Pointer and Puzzle Detective | Paid flow works end-to-end offline on iOS Safari, installed iOS PWA, Android Chrome and desktop; checkout passes the German withdrawal consent requirements |
| **1 · Validate** | Weeks 3–8 | Read r/Jigsawpuzzles and Facebook groups by hand for "cheating" and willingness-to-pay sentiment; one honest maker post; post 3 demo clips a week on TikTok, Reels and Shorts; publish 3 SEO pages; soft-launch Pro at the €9.99 founder price | ≥30% of new visitors activate (first successful scan); ≥40% of activated users use all free scans; first 50 paying buyers; refunds <5% |
| **2 · Launch** | Months 2–4 | Show HN; seed 20–30 micro/mid creators with Pro codes; contact 5–10 puzzle retailers about QR cards and affiliate deals; start the Play closed test, then publish the TWA; A/B test 3-scan quota vs "first puzzle free"; move to the €12.99 standard price | Paywall-view→purchase ≥3%, and activated→paid ≥2% (the freemium benchmark); at least one video >100K views; Play listing live |
| **3 · Scale or stop** | Months 4–9 | If iOS is >40% of paying traffic and conversion holds, build Capacitor iOS with native camera and haptics; add regional prices; completion-screen affiliate link and tip; pitch Ravensburger on grid-reference data; test small Meta web-conversion ads ($50–200) using the best organic clip, measured as cost per *activated* user | Cost per paying user < ~€7 before scaling paid; €1,000/month net is the "worth continuing" line, which **17.3% of new subscription apps reach within two years** ([SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)) |

The kill or pivot signal is **activated→paid below 1% after 1,000 activated users, with refunds above 8%**. That combination points to an accuracy problem or a stigma problem, not a pricing problem. The response is to go back to Phase 0 accuracy work or to the sentiment research, not to cut the price toward PuzzleLens's $0.99 level.

## Risks and how sure we are

| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Fast commoditisation and clones (≥5 entrants in 2026, plus an open-source PWA) | High / high | Close or licence the repo; compete on multi-piece batches, fit and patches, and accuracy on the sky; move fast while every competitor has 0–1 ratings |
| Accuracy failures on hard puzzles cause refunds and bad reviews | Medium / high | Gate after a *successful* scan; "unsure" warnings; try-next loop; publish honest limits |
| "Cheating" stigma suppresses adoption or provokes community backlash | Unknown (Reddit unread) / high | Assistant framing, graded hints, self-placement statistics; validate in Phase 1 |
| GitHub Pages terms and the cost of changing origin later | Medium / medium | Move hosting and domain before launch |
| Client-side gate bypassed | High / low | Accept it; signed tokens keep honest buyers honest |
| iOS storage eviction and Safari/PWA storage split lose purchases | Medium / medium | Email-based restore; token re-issue from the Worker |
| Store policy (Apple 4.2, Play billing rules for TWAs, outcome of US Supreme Court case) | Medium / medium | Web first; stores are optional add-ons |
| MoR fee and product changes (Lemon Squeezy → Stripe migration, conflicting Polar rates) | Medium / low | Choose a provider with documented licence keys; check rates on vendor pages |
| German compliance (Widerrufsrecht, Impressum, Kleinunternehmer treatment of MoR payouts) | Low with an MoR / medium | MoR checkout; Steuerberater review |

Evidence quality varies a lot across this report. **Strong**: competitor prices and features from the stores themselves; store fee schedules (Apple newsroom, Google coverage); German withdrawal law; RevenueCat's hard-paywall vs freemium and trial-length findings. Note that these benchmarks come from app-store subscription apps, **not PWAs**, and web purchases make up only 3–5% of revenue even for the apps that offer them. **Medium**: ad RPM ranges, CPI and Apple Search Ads costs, creator audience sizes, affiliate commission rates. **Weak or inferred**: any conversion rate for this product; the choice between quota and first-puzzle-free; the TikTok playbook, which rests on one secondary case study; demographic skew among buyers; MoR exact rates; licence-API CORS behaviour; whether Play Billing is required in a TWA; the GitHub Pages and no-LICENSE points, which come from background knowledge. **Missing entirely and worth validating by hand**: Reddit and Facebook sentiment on solver apps, paying and cheating; search volume for target queries; PuzPal's price and Puzzle Detective's trial terms; any head-to-head accuracy comparison; non-US competitor pricing.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the main decision is. Benchmarks settle "subscription or one-time" fairly clearly for an episodic tool with no server costs. The harder questions are whether puzzlers accept an assistant at all, and whether Puzzle Solver's accuracy on the sky justifies a price. Neither can be answered by desk research, so the cheapest high-value step is a few weeks of reading r/Jigsawpuzzles by hand and posting demo videos *before* writing payment code. The comments on those videos are the market research nobody has done yet.

The window is short, and the advantage is structural. Competitors meter or charge per piece because they locate one piece per photo, and some pay for servers and OpenAI calls. Puzzle Solver locates 40 pieces in one photo at zero marginal cost, so it can make "unlimited, private, pay once" its whole identity, which per-search rivals cannot copy without rebuilding their product. That advantage lasts only if the code is not handed to the next clone and the app is not tied to a GitHub Pages origin before its first paying user arrives.
